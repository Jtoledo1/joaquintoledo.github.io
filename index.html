Research

Property Taxes as Market Signals: Evidence from Housing Markets with Information Frictions
Hugo E. Silva, Joaquín Toledo Tesoro t_id=5313632" target="_blank">SSRN

Using a regression discontinuity design on georeferenced data from Santiago, this paper shows that property tax liability increases housing prices by about 4%. The results suggest that tax liability acts as a certification mechanism that reduces information asymmetry, reversing standard negative capitalization effects in opaque markets.

Property Taxes and Rental Prices: Evidence from a Large-Scale Cadastral Reassessment
Matias Brum, Joaquín Toledo Tesoro t_id=6072726" target="_blank">SSRN

Exploiting a cadastral reassessment in Uruguay, this paper finds that property tax increases raise rental prices by 9.3%, with a pass-through of about 44%. The results imply inelastic housing supply and highlight the role of institutional frictions in shaping tax incidence.

When Do Land-Use Regulations Matter? Stringency, Compliance, and Urban Development in Santiago
Joaquín Toledo Tesoro, Diego Gil, Hugo Silva #">PDF (coming soon)

This paper studies when land-use regulations bind using detailed data from Santiago. FAR constraints are consistently binding, while height limits only matter under strong enforcement. In low-compliance areas, height restrictions generate uncertainty and are discounted in prices.
